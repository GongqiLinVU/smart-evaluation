II. My Observations

1. Rule-based is generous on surface features, LLM is stricter on depth.

The rule-based engine gives high scores for things it can count — "47 configuration keywords" = 100/100, "7 design patterns mentioned" = 100/100. But it can't judge whether those mentions are meaningful. The LLM correctly identifies that patterns are "mentioned but not explained in depth."

2. LLM is very consistent but too coarse.

Both LLM runs returned identical 18/18/18. That's good for reliability, but the 5-level scale (6/12/18/24/30) means every criterion jumps in 6-point increments — a student scoring 17/30 looks identical to one scoring 13/30. There's no nuance within a level.

3. Neither captures what a tutor would see.

A tutor who supervised this project might know:

- The student contributed strongly to the team's architecture discussions
- The demo went well and showed working code
- The student iterated on feedback from earlier drafts
- The document structure improved significantly from v1 to v2

None of that lives in the document text.





III. My Recommendation: "Tutor Review Dimensions"

I think we need a Tutor Interaction Review layer — not just score adjustment (which we have), but structured qualitative input that becomes part of the final grade. Here's what I'm thinking:

- Flexible scoring scale — Allow scores at any integer (0-30), not just the 5 fixed levels
- Tutor review dimensions — Configurable additional criteria tutors can rate (e.g., "Presentation & Demo", "Iterative Improvement", "Group Contribution", "Critical Thinking")
- Weighted final score — Combine rule-based + LLM + tutor reviews with configurable weights (e.g., 30% rule-based, 30% LLM, 40% tutor)
- Tutor notes per criterion — Not just a global score adjustment, but per-criterion comments like "Student explained architecture well in the demo, upgrading from COMPETENT to PROFICIENT"
This gives you the balance: automation handles the objective/structural analysis, the LLM handles semantic depth, and the tutor handles the human context that neither can see.
